# CH09. Interpreting QCD and Projected P&L, and Iterative Validation

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- Check whether a pricing or business hypothesis is executable from a QCD (Quality·Cost·Delivery) perspective.
- Distinguish Projected P&L and Effective Market as two numbers that answer different questions.
- Explain why estimating the Effective Market must be an iterative, repeated check rather than a single calculation.

## 2. Core Concepts

QCD stands for Quality, Cost, and Delivery — three factors reviewed together once a hypothesis has been set, to confirm whether it is actually executable. Quality asks whether you can actually produce a product or service at the level the target customer requires. Delivery asks whether you can supply it at the scale and timing the market demands. Cost asks — on the premise that quality and delivery are achievable — whether you can produce them at a cost you can afford.

Projected P&L and Effective Market can both be expressed as price × sales volume, but they answer different questions. Projected P&L is closer to the question "given the resources currently secured, or securable within a given period, how much can actually be sold?" — it is strongly constrained by immediately available resources such as production capacity, headcount, channels, and marketing budget. Effective Market, by contrast, includes the potential area reachable with the current product and business model once additional resources are secured, even if that area cannot be captured right now due to a lack of resources.

Estimating the Effective Market is not a prophecy that predicts the future precisely — it is work that demonstrates a result derived by consistently applying assumptions and logic at the present point in time. This is why the 9-step Effective Market estimation is not a sequence that ends once you pass through it, but a nonlinear validation process in which realistic constraints discovered in later steps send you back to earlier steps to readjust assumptions.

## 3. Why It Matters

If even one element of QCD is not satisfied, no matter how attractive the positioning and pricing hypothesis, it will not hold up at the execution stage. If quality is achievable but the cost does not work, or the cost works but delivery cannot happen at the desired time, the hypothesis is not yet an executable model. In this case, you should not simply accept the QCD check result — you need to go back to the hypotheses on targeting, positioning, distribution, and price and readjust them. In other words, QCD is not a single gate that passes or fails a hypothesis once — it is closer to a feedback mechanism that continuously adjusts the hypothesis to fit reality.

If Projected P&L and Effective Market are not distinguished and are used interchangeably, the realistic executability question — "can we survive with this plan right now?" — gets mixed up with the scalability question — "how far can this business model expand once more resources are secured?" The portion that can be immediately monetized with current resources, i.e., the part close to Projected P&L, can be viewed as close to SOM, while the Effective Market that can logically be reached with additional resources can be explained as SAM. Without distinguishing the two numbers, the realistic revenue plan and the growth potential become indistinguishable — creating the risk of mistaking a plan the business cannot even sustain today for expansion potential, or conversely undervaluing a business with real growth potential based solely on today's resource constraints.

If the Effective Market estimate is fixed as a single correct answer, it fails to reflect the fact that the market, competitive environment, customer behavior, cost, and resources keep changing. What matters is not only the precision of the calculated number, but whether the assumptions and decisions behind that number can be explained.

## 4. Framework / Logic

The QCD check is not a procedure that passes each element one at a time in sequence — it is a check that looks at all three together, simultaneously.

- Quality: Can you actually produce a product or service at the level the target customer requires?
- Delivery: Can you supply it at the scale and timing the market demands?
- Cost: On the premise that quality and delivery are achievable, can you produce them at a cost you can afford?

If even one of the three elements is not satisfied, go back at that point to the hypothesis stage (targeting, positioning, distribution, price) and readjust the assumptions. This structure shares the same underlying principle as the iterative structure of the full 9-step Effective Market estimation process.

Projected P&L and Effective Market should be read as distinct, as follows.

| Category | Question | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Projected P&L | Given resources currently secured or securable within a given period, how much can actually be sold? | Current executability, close to SOM |
| Effective Market | How far can the potential area reachable with the current product and business model extend once additional resources are secured? | Scalability, explainable as SAM |

The 9-step Effective Market estimation is not a linear procedure that ends once completed — it is a nonlinear process that is refined by returning to earlier steps depending on the situation with cost and resources. Rather than fixing a single number as the correct answer, the proper use of this methodology is to recalculate the Effective Market every time an assumption about customers, price, channel, resources, or cost changes.

## 5. Application Process

1. Once a hypothesis is set, immediately check QCD together. First confirm whether Quality and Delivery are achievable, and on that premise, confirm whether Cost is affordable.
2. If any one of QCD is blocked, record which element it was blocked on, and go back to the targeting, positioning, distribution, or price hypothesis to make an adjustment.
3. Based on a hypothesis that has passed QCD, separately estimate Projected P&L (the scale that can actually be sold based on current resources) and Effective Market (the scale reachable once additional resources are secured).
4. Do not merge the two numbers into one. Use Projected P&L to judge current executability, and Effective Market to judge scalability.
5. If even one assumption about customers, price, channel, resources, or cost changes, go back to the earlier step affected by that assumption and redo the QCD check and Effective Market estimation. Do not treat this as a one-time process — repeat it.

## 6. Applying This in Pricing or the Harness

The QCD executability check is the conceptual foundation on which the BEP (break-even point) calculation feature of the Pricing Harness, covered later in Part III, stands. This is because the cost affordability review confirmed in QCD is exactly the problem that break-even calculation addresses. Likewise, the principle that the Effective Market estimate must be validated repeatedly (KM063) is the conceptual foundation of the Harness's Scenario Compare feature. The principle that the Effective Market must be recalculated every time an assumption changes is the starting point for a feature that compares multiple scenarios side by side. ## 7. Practical Checkpoints

- Are you confirming a hypothesis after checking only one element of QCD, without reviewing the other two?
- Are you reviewing Cost before, or independently of, Quality and Delivery? Cost is an element that should be reviewed on the premise that quality and delivery are achievable.
- Are you reporting the Projected P&L number and the Effective Market number mixed together as a single "revenue outlook"?
- Are you using the Effective Market estimate as-is after calculating it once, without revisiting it? Is this a number that has not been recalculated even though the assumptions have changed?
- Are you presenting the Effective Market estimation result as "the correct answer for predicting the future"? This number is closer to a demonstration that explains assumptions and logic.

## 8. Key Takeaways

QCD is a procedure for checking, simultaneously, from the perspectives of quality, cost, and delivery, whether a hypothesis is executable once it has been set — if even one element is blocked, you must go back to the hypothesis stage and adjust. Projected P&L and Effective Market use the same formula (price × sales volume) but are separate numbers that answer different questions — current executability and scalability — and should not be mixed. Estimating the Effective Market is not a prophecy that predicts the future, but work that demonstrates a result obtained by consistently applying assumptions and logic; the 9-step process is an iterative validation process in which constraints found in later steps send you back to earlier steps to readjust assumptions.

## 9. Practical Questions

- Among Quality, Cost, and Delivery, which element is most uncertain in the hypothesis you are currently reviewing, and what evidence will you use to confirm it?
- Is the revenue number you are currently reporting closer to Projected P&L or to Effective Market? Are you clearly communicating that distinction to decision-makers?
- When did you most recently recalculate the Effective Market estimate, and are there any changed assumptions (customer, price, channel, resources, cost) since then that have not yet been reflected?
