# CH01-EX01. Monthly Minimum Sales Target of a Hypothetical Cafe (Total Fixed Costs of 5,900,000 KRW)

- Related Chapter: CH01 (Price Is the Outcome of the Business Model) §10 "Fixed Costs and the Monthly Minimum Sales Target for Small Businesses"
- Data source: [Practice-only hypothetical data] — practice assumptions from the Standard Lecture Plan 3-1 AI-assisted cost management and revenue model workshop. Not data from an actual business.
- Note: This case uses the workshop's simple formula (profit per unit = selling price − variable cost per unit). VAT and the fee basis are not reflected, so if the selling price includes VAT, subtract the VAT first (see CH10).

## Case Data

The hypothetical cafe's monthly fixed costs are as follows.

| Category | Item | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Living-expense fixed costs | Housing 700,000 + food 600,000 + insurance and medical 250,000 + education 300,000 + communication 100,000 + transportation 150,000 + other 100,000 | Total (Question 1) |
| Business fixed costs | Rent and management fees 1,200,000 + salaried staff pay 1,800,000 + loan interest and insurance 250,000 + communication and utilities 300,000 + other 150,000 | Total (Question 1) |

The selling prices and variable cost per unit (materials, packaging, etc.) of the three main products are as follows.

| Product | Selling price | Variable cost per unit |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee | 5,000 KRW | 2,000 KRW |
| Sandwich | 7,500 KRW | 3,100 KRW |
| Dessert | 6,000 KRW | 2,100 KRW |

## Think it through

1. How much are the living-expense fixed costs, the business fixed costs, and the total fixed costs? What percentage of total fixed costs are the living-expense fixed costs?
2. What are the profit per unit and the profit rate for each of the three products?
3. If only coffee is sold, what is the monthly minimum sales volume in cups? How many cups would it take to also leave 500,000 KRW of monthly net profit?
4. If the sales mix is coffee 60% : sandwich 25% : dessert 15%, what are the average profit per unit and the monthly minimum sales volume (including the quantity of each product)?

## What this case illustrates

It shows that if you add up fixed costs including living expenses and divide that total by profit per unit, you get "the minimum number of units to sell in a month." This formula has the same structure as CH13's break-even sales volume (fixed costs ÷ unit contribution margin).

## Calculation

1. Living-expense fixed costs = 700,000 + 600,000 + 250,000 + 300,000 + 100,000 + 150,000 + 100,000 = **2,200,000 KRW**
   Business fixed costs = 1,200,000 + 1,800,000 + 250,000 + 300,000 + 150,000 = **3,700,000 KRW**
   Total fixed costs = **5,900,000 KRW**. Share of living-expense fixed costs = 2,200,000 ÷ 5,900,000 = **about 37.3%**
2. Profit per unit = selling price − variable cost per unit

| Product | Profit per unit | Profit rate |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee | 5,000 − 2,000 = **3,000 KRW** | 60.0% |
| Sandwich | 7,500 − 3,100 = **4,400 KRW** | 58.7% |
| Dessert | 6,000 − 2,100 = **3,900 KRW** | 65.0% |

3. Monthly minimum sales volume = 5,900,000 ÷ 3,000 = 1,966.7 → **1,967 cups** (rounded up). Adding the net profit target gives (5,900,000 + 500,000) ÷ 3,000 = 2,133.3 → **2,134 cups** (rounded up).
4. Average profit per unit = 0.60×3,000 + 0.25×4,400 + 0.15×3,900 = 1,800 + 1,100 + 585 = **3,485 KRW**
   Monthly minimum sales volume = 5,900,000 ÷ 3,485 = 1,692.97 → **1,693 units**. Split by the mix, that is about 1,016 coffees, about 423 sandwiches, and about 254 desserts.

## Interpretation

- This quantity is only the "minimum" target that barely covers fixed costs; growth requires a higher target.
- Putting living-expense fixed costs into business fixed costs is the workshop's way of setting a realistic target for small business owners, and it should be read as distinct from accounting cost treatment.
