# CH04-EX05. From List Price to Profit: Filling in the Price Structure Table

- Related Chapter: CH04. How Channel and Customer Relationship Reshape Price Structure
- Related MN/KM: MN06 / KM048

## Think it through

[Practice-only hypothetical data] A discount program of 20% is applied to a product with a recommended consumer price (List price) of 20,000 KRW. Cost is 55% of the Sales price and SG&A is 20% of the Sales price.

1. Find the Sales price, cost, SG&A, total cost, and profit with its profit rate, in that order. Check that the sum works (Sales price = total cost + profit).
2. To raise the target profit rate to 30% of the Sales price, what must total cost be at most? If SG&A is left as is, how much cost is allowed?
3. If profit comes out negative, which line of the price structure should you check first?

<details>
<summary>Show answer and walkthrough</summary>

1. Sales price = 20,000 × (1 − 0.2) = 16,000 KRW, cost = 16,000 × 55% = 8,800 KRW, SG&A = 16,000 × 20% = 3,200 KRW, total cost = 12,000 KRW (75%), profit = 16,000 − 12,000 = 4,000 KRW (25%). Check: 4,000 + 12,000 = 16,000.
2. Target profit = 16,000 × 30% = 4,800 KRW, so total cost must be 11,200 KRW or less. If SG&A of 3,200 KRW is kept, cost is allowed up to 11,200 − 3,200 = 8,000 KRW (50% of the Sales price).
3. There is no single correct answer. Check line by line whether the discount program is excessive and whether the cost ratio and SG&A ratio are higher than assumed. At the same time, see whether SG&A was mixed into cost in the calculation.

- What this case illustrates: A practice exercise in filling in a price structure table, in which the discount program is subtracted from the List price to obtain the Sales price, and the Sales price is split into total cost and profit. All ratios are based on the Sales price, and the profit here is the value after subtracting SG&A as well, which differs from contribution margin.
