# CH03-EX01. Equilibrium Price and Excess Supply in a Hypothetical Market

- Related Chapter: CH03. Two Logics of Price Decisions — Customer Value and Competition
- Related MN/KM: MN06 / KM046

## Think it through

[Practice-only hypothetical data] This is a hypothetical market for explanation. It is not an actual market's demand function.

- Quantity demanded = 3,000 − 0.2 × price
- Quantity supplied = 0.3 × price − 500

1. What are the equilibrium price and the equilibrium quantity?
2. What state is the market in when the price is 10,000 KRW? And at 5,000 KRW?
3. Write two paths a seller can choose when facing unsold inventory at 10,000 KRW, and write one thing this supply-and-demand model cannot explain.

<details>
<summary>Show answer and walkthrough</summary>

1. Quantity demanded = quantity supplied, so 3,000 − 0.2P = 0.3P − 500, that is, 3,500 = 0.5P. The equilibrium price is 7,000 KRW, and the equilibrium quantity is 3,000 − 1,400 = 1,600 units (on the supply side too, 2,100 − 500 = 1,600 units).
2. At 10,000 KRW: quantity demanded is 1,000 units and quantity supplied is 2,500 units, so there is excess supply of 1,500 units. At 5,000 KRW: quantity demanded is 2,000 units and quantity supplied is 1,000 units, so there is excess demand of 1,000 units.
3. The options are to lower the price toward equilibrium, or to add differentiated value and defend the high price (there is no single fixed answer). This model fits well when there are many sellers and the product is the same. It cannot explain situations where a differentiated product, channel, or customer relationship lets the same product sell at different prices.
