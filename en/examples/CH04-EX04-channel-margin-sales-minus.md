# CH04-EX04. Margin by Channel Stage and Sales Minus Allowable Cost

- Related Chapter: CH04. How Channel and Customer Relationship Reshape Price Structure
- Related MN/KM: MN06 / KM048

## Think it through

[Practice-only hypothetical data] A product with a consumer price of 30,000 KRW (assumed to exclude VAT) reaches the consumer through retail and wholesale. The retail margin is 35% and the wholesale margin is 12%, and both margin rates are based on the selling price of the respective stage. The manufacturer's target profit rate is 20% and the SG&A ratio is 15%, and both ratios are based on the manufacturer's supply price.

1. What are the retail margin amount and the retail purchase price?
2. What are the wholesale margin amount and the manufacturer's supply price? What percentage of the consumer price is the supply price?
3. If you subtract the target profit and SG&A from the supply price, what is the allowable cost?
4. If the actual sourcing cost is 11,500 KRW, is sourcing feasible? If not, what adjustments could be considered?

<details>
<summary>Show answer and walkthrough</summary>

1. Retail margin amount = 30,000 × 35% = 10,500 KRW, retail purchase price = 30,000 − 10,500 = 19,500 KRW.
2. Wholesale margin amount = 19,500 × 12% = 2,340 KRW, manufacturer's supply price = 19,500 − 2,340 = 17,160 KRW. Relative to the consumer price, 17,160 ÷ 30,000 = 57.2%.
3. Target profit = 17,160 × 20% = 3,432 KRW, SG&A = 17,160 × 15% = 2,574 KRW, so allowable cost = 17,160 − 3,432 − 2,574 = 11,154 KRW.
4. The actual cost of 11,500 KRW is 346 KRW higher than the allowable cost, so the target profit cannot be protected under the current structure. You must choose among cost reduction, changing the composition, defending the price through differentiation, and revisiting the target rate; there is no single correct answer. The key is to write the reasons together.

- What this case illustrates: A Sales minus calculation in which channel margins are subtracted stage by stage from the consumer price to obtain the manufacturer's supply price, and the target profit and SG&A are then subtracted from it to obtain the allowable cost. It confirms that the base of the margin rate (selling price basis) must be unified for the numbers to add up.
