# CH02-EX01. Same Indirect Cost, Different Allocation Basis (Hypothetical Case of 1,000,000 KRW Manufacturing Overhead)

- Related Chapter: CH02 (Cost and Cost Structure) §10 "Direct Costs, Indirect Costs, and Allocation"
- Data source: [Practice-only hypothetical data] — practice assumptions from the Standard Lecture Plan 3-1 cost theory deck. Not data from an actual company.

## Case Data

This month's total manufacturing overhead for a small factory making products A, B, and C is **1,000,000 KRW**. This amount cannot be measured separately by product, so a basis is to be set and the amount allocated across the three products.

| Product | Direct labor hours | Machine hours | Units produced |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 200 hours | 500 hours | 250 units |
| B | 300 hours | 300 hours | 150 units |
| C | 500 hours | 200 hours | 100 units |

## Think it through

1. If allocated on direct labor hours, what are the allocated amount and the allocation per unit for each product?
2. If allocated on machine hours, what are the allocated amount and the allocation per unit for each product?
3. By what factor does product A's allocation per unit change depending on the basis? What caution does this result call for in price decisions?

## What this case illustrates

It shows that even when the total amount of an indirect cost is fixed, how much belongs to each product depends on the allocation basis. The allocation basis is chosen by the company, and there is no single correct answer.

## Calculation

- Total labor hours are 1,000 and total machine hours are 1,000, so the allocation rate per hour is 1,000 KRW under both bases.

| Product | Allocation on labor hours | Per unit | Allocation on machine hours | Per unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 200,000 KRW | **800 KRW** | 500,000 KRW | **2,000 KRW** |
| B | 300,000 KRW | **2,000 KRW** | 300,000 KRW | **2,000 KRW** |
| C | 500,000 KRW | **5,000 KRW** | 200,000 KRW | **2,000 KRW** |
| Total | 1,000,000 KRW | | 1,000,000 KRW | |

- Product A's allocation per unit changes from 800 KRW to 2,000 KRW, a factor of **2.5**. Product C's drops from 5,000 KRW to 2,000 KRW.

## Interpretation

- The total of the allocation results is the same 1,000,000 KRW under either basis; what changes is each product's share.
- So when setting a price based on product cost, or judging which product leaves money behind, you must record together which basis was used for allocation.
- The two bases are examples for practice, and the source material says only that "there are various allocation methods."
