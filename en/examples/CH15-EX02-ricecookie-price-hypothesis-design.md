# CH15-EX02. Writing a One-Sentence Price Hypothesis and Four Validation Design Cells — The 18,000 KRW Rice Cookie Increase Option

- **Related Chapter**: CH15. Validating Price Hypotheses and Decision-Making (§10 Deep Dive)
- **Related Tool**: None (Pricing Harness has no feature to store validation data. CH15 §6). Only the numerical basis is calculated with the same formulas as MODE A, BEP, and Volume Profit.
- **Source**: The rice cookie case used in Standard Lecture Plan 3-1-02 (Practice 4) and 3-1-12 (Practice 5). A hypothetical teaching case [Practice-only hypothetical data]; not actual product or channel conditions.

## Situation

A handmade rice cookie 6-piece set (hypothetical) is currently sold at 15,000 KRW (VAT-inclusive). Direct cost is 6,000 KRW, delivery is 3,000 KRW, the fee is 5% of the amount actually paid, and the monthly fixed operating cost is 600,000 KRW. You are considering raising the price to 18,000 KRW. Selling 250 sets a month at the current price gives an operating profit of about 371,591 KRW.

## Think it through

1. Write this increase option as a one-sentence price hypothesis whose truth or falsity can be decided.
2. Separate and write down the numbers that calculation confirms and the assumptions that can be confirmed only in the market.
3. Write the four validation design cells (research and verification target, measurement metric and decision criterion, method, target and period, feedback data). Set the reference numbers yourself.
4. Check whether the four cells are connected.

There is no single correct answer. What follows is one example, and the reference numbers must be set by each business itself (CH15 §4).

## 1. Price hypothesis

"Current customers will buy even at 18,000 KRW."

## 2. Separating calculation from assumption

| Category | Content | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution margin | 3,886.36 KRW at 15,000 KRW → 6,463.64 KRW at 18,000 KRW | Calculation |
| Break-even quantity | about 154.4 sets → about 92.8 sets | Calculation |
| Quantity needed for the same operating profit | The operating profit of 371,591 KRW at 15,000 KRW and 250 sets becomes almost the same at about 150 sets at 18,000 KRW (18,000 KRW and 150 sets: 369,545 KRW) | Calculation |
| 150 or more sets a month sell at 18,000 KRW | An assumption that customers will buy even at the raised price | **Assumption** (verification target) |

The calculation tells you "how far the quantity may fall." "How far it actually falls" can be known only in the market.

## 3. Example validation design

| Cell | Example |
|---|---|
| Research and verification target | Do existing customers buy even at 18,000 KRW? |
| Measurement metric and decision criterion | Monthly sales quantity at the raised price. Set the decision criterion yourself (e.g., keep if 150 sets or more, where calculated operating profit becomes similar; revise or discard if below). 150 sets is a criterion that came from the calculation, not a statistical criterion |
| Method, target, and period | A trial sale for a set period, at a reversible scale, in one channel and one segment rather than all customers |
| Feedback data | Record sales quantity, discount requests, and reasons for refusal, and use them as the basis for judging whether assumptions other than price (cost, value, channel) need to be reopened |

## 4. Connection check

- Can the measurement metric actually be observed and recorded? Monthly sales quantity can be recorded.
- Is there a decision criterion (keep / revise / discard)? The example above splits them on one numeric criterion.
- Is it a small, reversible experiment? It is limited to one channel and one segment.
- Since the current tool has no feature to store validation records, keep them in a separate document such as a worksheet (CH15 §6).
